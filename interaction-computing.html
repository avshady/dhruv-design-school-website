Who it is for

Completed design/engineering study or equivalent portfolio-based experience. Assess research, logical reasoning and basic digital skills. Programming beginners receive a bridge within Block 1.

This advanced programme expects relevant prior study or practical experience. It is not a beginner pathway.

What you will study

Block 1 / 10 weeks

Human-centred research, interaction principles, interface/accessibility basics and prototyping. Programming foundations, low-voltage circuit literacy, tool safety and systematic debugging.

Block 2 / 10 weeks

Microcontrollers, sensors, outputs and simple actuators; state-based behaviour and data capture. Physical/interface sketches, breadboards, soldering authorisation and rapid user tests.

Block 3 / 10 weeks

Integrate enclosure, electronics and interface; connectivity fundamentals, privacy, consent and reliability. Prototype connected services; critically test any AI component and design failure states.

Block 4 / 10 weeks

Build an interactive product or installation for a real context. Define success measures, conduct usability and accessibility testing, log faults and prepare repeatable assembly/documentation.

Industry / 8 weeks

Externally mentored live deployment trial or placement. Track field behaviour, maintenance and user feedback; revise the system and present a working demonstration with handover documentation.

What you will make

Three interaction case studies; two physical prototypes; one field-tested final system; interface flows, code repository, wiring/BOM, assembly guide, user-test evidence and demo video.

Illustrative material study. This is not a photograph of student work.

Professional readiness

Demonstrate a working system, reproduce its setup from documentation and diagnose an introduced fault. Final review must include user evidence, accessibility and safe failure behaviour.

Potential career directions

Junior interaction designer, creative-technology associate or prototyping assistant. Further engineering validation is needed before selling a regulated or safety-critical product.

Rubric: technical craft 25%, research 15%, iteration 20%, final project 30%, professional practice 10%. Graduate at 70% overall, with technical craft and final project each at least 70%; all safety checks passed and 85% guided attendance. Rework before progression. Career directions describe possible pathways, not guaranteed employment.

How you will learn

40 studio weeks: 24 guided + 12 independent hours/week; then 8 industry weeks at 35 hours/week. Total: 960 guided + 480 independent + 280 industry = 1,720 hours; approximately 4 weeks of breaks/transitions.

A planned low-voltage electronics studio, extracted soldering stations, component storage and shared digital prototyping facilities.

Learning combines demonstrations, supervised making, independent development and critique. Progression depends on demonstrated skill, with rework and feedback where needed. Facilities and faculty appointments will be confirmed before enrolment.
