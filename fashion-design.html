Who it is for

Class 12 or equivalent; interview, observational drawing and a simple making task. Beginners welcome. Portfolio assessed for curiosity and process, not polish.

Beginner applicants are welcome. Selection will consider curiosity, observation, commitment and willingness to learn through making.

What you will study

Term 1 / 18 weeks

Shared design foundation: observation, drawing, colour, form, design history and critique. Textile behaviour, hand stitches, machine safety, seams, measurements and basic blocks.

Term 2 / 18 weeks

Design and construction taught together: pattern manipulation, draping, fit corrections, garment finishing, fashion illustration and technical flats. Make and refit four garment types.

Term 3 / 18 weeks

Collection research, Indian textile/craft contexts, surface development, introductory knit/stretch construction, digital patterns and 3D sampling. Sourcing, costing, quality and repairability.

Term 4 / 18 weeks

Self-directed six-look collection: iterative toiles, fittings and final construction. Tech packs, size specifications, range planning, photography, authorship/IP and a small-label business brief.

Industry / 12 weeks

Supervised placement or an institute-run live client studio with an external mentor. Maintain a work log, deliver production files and defend the final portfolio before a mixed faculty/industry jury.

What you will make

Six finished collection looks; four earlier assessed garments; pattern/toile archive; three complete tech packs; costing sheet; process journal and photographed portfolio. Document personal work within team projects.

Professional readiness

Independently draft, cut, sew and fit an unfamiliar garment brief. Final collection must demonstrate construction quality, a coherent concept and a usable production handover.

Potential career directions

Junior fashion designer, design/sampling assistant or product-development assistant; further mentored practice for independent label ownership.

Rubric: technical craft 25%, research 15%, iteration 20%, final project 30%, professional practice 10%. Graduate at 70% overall, with technical craft and final project each at least 70%; all safety checks passed and 85% guided attendance. Rework before progression. Career directions describe possible pathways, not guaranteed employment.

How you will learn

72 studio weeks: 24 guided + 12 independent hours/week; then 12 industry weeks at 35 hours/week. Total: 1,728 guided + 864 independent + 420 industry = 3,012 hours. Remaining calendar time: breaks and transitions.

A planned fashion studio with sewing, cutting, pressing and fitting areas, supported by digital design access and secure project storage.

Learning combines demonstrations, supervised making, independent development and critique. Progression depends on demonstrated skill, with rework and feedback where needed. Facilities and faculty appointments will be confirmed before enrolment.
