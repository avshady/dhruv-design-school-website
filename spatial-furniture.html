Who it is for

Class 12 or equivalent; interview, spatial observation and model-making task. Basic maths required; applicants do not need prior architecture or CAD training.

Beginner applicants are welcome. Selection will consider curiosity, observation, commitment and willingness to learn through making.

What you will study

Term 1 / 18 weeks

Shared design foundation: observation, form, colour, history and critique. Human scale, measured drawing, spatial composition, model-making, material literacy and workshop safety.

Term 2 / 18 weeks

Survey and documentation, residential/small commercial layouts, furniture ergonomics, joinery and hardware. CAD plans/sections, digital models, material specification and inclusive circulation.

Term 3 / 18 weeks

Furniture prototyping, production drawings, lighting/acoustics principles and building-services coordination. Read applicable access/fire requirements with specialists; prepare quantities, costs and schedules.

Term 4 / 18 weeks

Resolve one real small-space brief and one furniture family. Detail junctions, finishes, fixture schedules and budget; build a full-size piece, test it and revise before final presentation.

Industry / 12 weeks

Placement or externally mentored live studio with a design practice/fabricator. Join supervised site reviews, document changes and hand over drawings, specifications and a project report.

What you will make

Three spatial case studies; two furniture designs with one full-size prototype; measured survey; coordinated drawing/specification pack; quantity/cost schedule and fabrication/test journal.

Professional readiness

Produce a consistent plan-section-detail set and a buildable furniture drawing. Check dimensions, access and user fit; defend a tested prototype and budget against the original brief.

Potential career directions

Junior interior/spatial designer, furniture-design assistant or detailing associate. This programme does not confer architect registration or statutory sign-off authority.

Rubric: technical craft 25%, research 15%, iteration 20%, final project 30%, professional practice 10%. Graduate at 70% overall, with technical craft and final project each at least 70%; all safety checks passed and 85% guided attendance. Rework before progression. Career directions describe possible pathways, not guaranteed employment.

How you will learn

72 studio weeks: 24 guided + 12 independent hours/week; then 12 industry weeks at 35 hours/week. Total: 1,728 guided + 864 independent + 420 industry = 3,012 hours. Remaining calendar time: breaks and transitions.

A dedicated spatial/furniture studio, CAD access, materials library, model-making and prototype assembly. Full-size fabrication is planned with specialist partners.

Learning combines demonstrations, supervised making, independent development and critique. Progression depends on demonstrated skill, with rework and feedback where needed. Facilities and faculty appointments will be confirmed before enrolment.
