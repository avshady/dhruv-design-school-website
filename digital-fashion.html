Who it is for

Prior garment/pattern training or equivalent professional experience. Admission requires a garment, a pattern set and an explanation of fit corrections; basic computer fluency required.

This advanced programme expects relevant prior study or practical experience. It is not a beginner pathway.

What you will study

Weeks 1-4

Audit pattern and construction knowledge; digital file hygiene, measurements, technical flats and specification conventions. Establish a baseline physical sample for comparison.

Weeks 5-10

CLO or equivalent 3D garment workflow: pattern import/editing, sewing, avatars, fabric settings and fit review. Compare digital behaviour against physical fabric and garment tests.

Weeks 11-16

Tech packs: dimensions/tolerances, construction details, bill of materials, colourways, size specifications, grading rules, labels and revision control. Introduction to supplier/PLM handover.

Weeks 17-20

Build a digital capsule, plan variants and validate one physical sample. Review costing, sampling approvals, supplier feedback, file exchange and limitations of AI-assisted imagery.

Weeks 21-24

Externally reviewed production brief: submit digital garments and complete tech packs, respond to sample-room questions, correct discrepancies and defend the final handover.

What you will make

Six digital garment samples; three complete production tech packs; one physical validation sample; material/fit comparison records and a supplier-ready versioned file package.

Professional readiness

A pattern/sampling professional must be able to interpret the final pack without missing essential information. Reconcile digital and physical fit; trace every revision and verify dimensions manually.

Potential career directions

Junior 3D apparel developer, digital sampling assistant or technical-design assistant. This is an advanced extension of garment knowledge, not a beginner fashion qualification.

Rubric: technical craft 25%, research 15%, iteration 20%, final project 30%, professional practice 10%. Graduate at 70% overall, with technical craft and final project each at least 70%; all safety checks passed and 85% guided attendance. Rework before progression. Career directions describe possible pathways, not guaranteed employment.

How you will learn

24 teaching weeks: 12 guided + 12 independent hours/week = 288 guided + 288 independent = 576 hours. Includes a 4-week industry-reviewed final brief; approximately 2 weeks for breaks/transitions.

Digital workstations with appropriate software, plus access to sewing, cutting and fitting facilities to compare virtual samples with physical garments.

Learning combines demonstrations, supervised making, independent development and critique. Progression depends on demonstrated skill, with rework and feedback where needed. Facilities and faculty appointments will be confirmed before enrolment.
