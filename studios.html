A place to
work things out.

The planned campus brings complementary disciplines into a shared culture of experimentation, supported by technical instruction and responsible access to tools.

Studios that
start conversations.

A garment can lead to a material experiment. A furniture prototype can become an interaction study. Shared facilities should make those connections possible.

Our studio plan is being developed around small practical groups, technical supervision, project storage and the needs of sustained making.

Concept diagram only. Facilities are proposed and will be confirmed before enrolment.

01

Fashion & textiles

Planned for the opening phase

Pattern-making, draping, sewing, cutting and fit. An environment where design thinking and construction develop together.

02

Product & digital fabrication

Planned for the opening phase

CAD, model-making, material experiments and controlled FDM printing. Light prototypes on campus; specialist production with external workshops.

03

Spatial & furniture studio

Planned as capacity expands

Measured drawing, material libraries, scale models, furniture detailing and prototype assembly.

04

Interaction & physical computing

Later-phase facility

Low-voltage electronics, sensors, interfaces and functional prototypes, supported by trained technical staff.

05

Shared material & project library

Shared across disciplines

Materials to handle, processes to study and documented experiments to revisit. Secure storage and tool issue are part of the studio plan.

Better questions.
Real experiments.

What can a prototype teach us that a drawing cannot?

Our R&D ambition is to connect classroom enquiry with materials, manufacturing, Indian craft practices and everyday human needs. Research should leave a trail of evidence: observations, trials, tests and revisions.

Ideas worth investigating.

Illustrative directions for future projects—not completed research or existing partnerships.

What could offcuts become?

Investigate textile waste and material reuse through sampling, construction trials and practical performance tests.

Can a small space do more?

Explore adaptable furniture for compact spaces, with attention to comfort, repair and local fabrication.

Who is the interface leaving out?

Develop physical and digital interactions with diverse users, then test whether they are useful and understandable.
