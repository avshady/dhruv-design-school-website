Who it is for

Class 12 or equivalent; interview, measurement/numeracy check and manual-dexterity exercise. Beginners accepted. Experienced makers may seek module exemptions only through practical assessment.

Beginner applicants are welcome. Selection will consider curiosity, observation, commitment and willingness to learn through making.

What you will study

Block 1 / 10 weeks

Machine setup and safe operation, hand finishing, fabric identification, cutting discipline, seam samples and pressing. Measurements, garment terminology and basic pattern drafting.

Block 2 / 10 weeks

Bodice, skirt, sleeve and trouser blocks; pattern manipulation and draping. Toile construction, fittings on varied bodies, balance and ease; structured correction records.

Block 3 / 10 weeks

Shirts, trousers, dresses and simple jackets; linings, closures, facings and pockets. Introductory stretch construction, pattern digitisation, grading principles and cutting layouts.

Block 4 / 10 weeks

Work from technical flats/specifications; build a graded pattern set and repeatable sample. Size charts, material estimates, quality checks, alteration workflow and production documentation.

Industry / 8 weeks

Sampling-room placement or externally mentored production brief. Complete tracked samples to agreed specifications and deadlines, with fit review and an independent practical examination.

What you will make

Eight assessed garments across different construction problems; corrected block library; seam/finish sample book; one style graded across three sizes; fit logs, costing and production specification pack.

Illustrative material study. This is not a photograph of student work.

Professional readiness

Independently interpret a technical flat, make a pattern and sample, then correct fit. Complete an unseen pattern/measurement exercise and a quality-controlled repeat sample.

Potential career directions

Assistant pattern maker, sample machinist, garment-construction technician or fit/alteration assistant. Progression to senior pattern roles requires production experience.

Rubric: technical craft 25%, research 15%, iteration 20%, final project 30%, professional practice 10%. Graduate at 70% overall, with technical craft and final project each at least 70%; all safety checks passed and 85% guided attendance. Rework before progression. Career directions describe possible pathways, not guaranteed employment.

How you will learn

40 studio weeks: 24 guided + 12 independent hours/week; then 8 industry weeks at 35 hours/week. Total: 960 guided + 480 independent + 280 industry = 1,720 hours; approximately 4 weeks of breaks/transitions.

Reserved sewing and cutting stations, pressing and fitting areas, pattern storage and CAD access. Small practical groups and individual technical assessments.

Learning combines demonstrations, supervised making, independent development and critique. Progression depends on demonstrated skill, with rework and feedback where needed. Facilities and faculty appointments will be confirmed before enrolment.
